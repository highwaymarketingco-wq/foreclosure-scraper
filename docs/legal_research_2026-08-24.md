# Legal Research: NC & SC Distressed-Property Wholesaling Laws
**Date:** 2026-08-24
**Purpose:** Legal landscape for investor making cash offers at 10-50% of as-is value

## Summary Table

| Question | NC | SC |
|---|---|---|
| Legal to offer <50% of value? | ✅ Yes — no price floor | ✅ Yes — no price floor |
| Minimum-offer laws? | ❌ None | ❌ None |
| Anti-flipping restrictions? | ❌ None | ❌ None |
| Required disclosures? | ⚠️ Ch. 47E disclosure form (1-4 units); exemptions for foreclosures/estates | ⚠️ No mandatory form; licensees must reveal status |
| Contract assignment? | ⚠️ No explicit carve-out — higher risk. Market the contract, not the property | ✅ Explicit carve-out §40-57-30(44), §40-57-135(E)(1) |
| Cooling-off for sellers? | ❌ No seller cooling-off; 3-day buyer cancellation for missing disclosures only | ❌ None found |
| Penalty for unlicensed practice | Class 1 misdemeanor (§93A-8) | Up to $500/$1K/$10K (§40-57-725); misdemeanor for lapsed licensees |

## Key Findings

### 1. Offer Pricing — LEGAL in both states
- **No minimum-offer law** exists in NC or SC
- Offering 10-50 cents on the dollar is legal
- Risk is in *how* the offer is presented, not the price
- Fraud/duress/misrepresentation can void contracts under general contract law

### 2. Anti-Flipping — NO restrictions
- Buy, light cleanup (mow, trim, clean trash), resell as-is = no license needed
- Acting as principal (owner selling own property), not broker
- NC: §93A-2(c)(7) exempts owners selling own property
- SC: Owner selling own property is exempt from licensure

### 3. Contract Assignment
- **SC: SAFE** — §40-57-30(44) explicitly carves out "assigning a contractual right to purchase" from wholesaling definition. §40-57-135(E)(1) permits marketing contractual position (not the property itself)
- **NC: RISKY** — No explicit statutory carve-out. NCREC's position is that marketing a property under contract may constitute unlicensed brokerage. Safer to actually close (double closing) rather than assign

### 4. NC Required Disclosures (Chapter 47E)
- **§47E-4(a):** Must furnish Residential Property Disclosure Statement for 1-4 unit residential
- **§47E-4(b1):** Must furnish Owners' Association Disclosure if applicable
- **§47E-5(a):** Disclosure must be delivered no later than when purchaser makes offer
- **Exemptions (§47E-2(a)):** Court-ordered transfers, foreclosure sales, transfers by fiduciary/executor, tax sale transfers, transfers between co-owners, family transfers, government transfers
- **No "we buy houses" disclosure law** exists in NC

### 5. SC Disclosures
- No general mandatory seller disclosure form
- §40-57-135(F)(1): Licensed professionals must reveal license status in personal transactions (bold/underlined/capitalized on contract first page)
- Applies only to licensees, not unlicensed investors

### 6. Cooling-Off Periods
- **NC:** §47E-5(b) — 3-day buyer cancellation right if disclosures not delivered before offer. This is a BUYER's right, not seller's. No seller cooling-off period.
- **SC:** No cooling-off period or rescission right found
- **FTC Cooling-Off Rule** explicitly exempts real estate transactions
- Sellers are generally bound once they sign in both states

## Practical Implications

1. **Offer model is legal** — 10-50% of ARV cash offers are permitted
2. **Buy, cleanup, resell = no license needed** — principal, not broker
3. **SC is safer for contract assignment** — explicit statutory carve-out
4. **NC: actually close on the property** rather than assigning; if assigning, market the contract position only
5. **NC: provide disclosure form** when buying residential 1-4 units (or qualify for exemption)
6. **No seller cooling-off** — once they sign, they're bound
7. **Avoid fraud/duress/misrepresentation** — could void contract under general contract law
8. **Recommendation:** Provide written disclosure to seller that you are an investor intending to resell for profit, and that they are free to consult an attorney

## Statute References

### NC General Statutes
- **Chapter 93A** — Real Estate License Law
  - §93A-2(a): Defines "real estate broker"
  - §93A-2(c)(1): Owner exemption (own property)
  - §93A-2(c)(7): Individual owner selling own property exemption
  - §93A-6: Prohibited acts (fraud, misrepresentation)
  - §93A-8: Penalty — Class 1 misdemeanor for unlicensed practice
- **Chapter 47E** — Residential Property Disclosure Act
  - §47E-2(a): Exemptions (foreclosure, court-ordered, fiduciary, tax sale, family, government)
  - §47E-4(a): Residential Property Disclosure Statement required
  - §47E-4(b1): HOA/Mandatory Covenants Disclosure
  - §47E-4.1: Mineral rights disclosure
  - §47E-5(a): Disclosure delivery deadline
  - §47E-5(b): 3-day buyer cancellation right

### SC Code of Laws
- **Title 40, Chapter 57** — Real Estate License Law
  - §40-57-30(44): Defines "wholesaling" — carves out contract assignment
  - §40-57-135(E)(1): Permits marketing contractual position (not property)
  - §40-57-135(F)(1): Licensee must reveal status in personal transactions
  - §40-57-350(A): Prohibits licensed brokers from wholesaling
  - §40-57-710(A)(3)-(5): Disciplinary actions for misrepresentation
  - §40-57-725: Administrative penalties ($500/$1K/$10K)
  - §40-57-780: Misdemeanor for lapsed licensees
