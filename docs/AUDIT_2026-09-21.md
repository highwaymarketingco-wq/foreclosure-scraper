# Introspective audit, 2026-09-21

An audit of every source, county and signal on the foreclosure engine, for both lanes: foreclosures (flips) and distressed properties. It covers what is on the board, how fresh it is, whether the signals mean what they claim, how the pipeline actually runs, and where my own work this week was wrong.

Measured on the live board, 170,528 rows (NC 93,073, SC 77,455), 156 sources, on 2026-09-21 before the overnight rescore. Every count below was taken from the board or from a named file. Two independent read-only agents audited the scoring logic and the pipeline operations; their full reports are `docs/audit_signal_logic_2026-09-21.md` (in git) and a local-only operations report that names credential and data locations and is kept out of git like the other held-out docs. Where I cite an F-number it is from the first report, an O-number from the second, an A-number is mine.

## 1. Executive summary

**Verdict.** The board is large and mostly well-populated on identity, value and mail inside the 18 footprint counties. It is a much weaker instrument than its size suggests, for four reasons that compound:

1. **The tier rewards stacking, not events.** The scorer reads no date at all. A foreclosure whose sale was 200 days ago can stay HOT; a closed upset-bid window still scores 22 points; a stayed foreclosure ranks higher because a bankruptcy match adds a category (F2, F3). Only 100 of the 1,646 HOT leads (6%) are foreclosure-lane types, the tier has no days-to-sale term (F7), and WARM is at least 71,010 leads (over 40% of the board), so it does not discriminate.
2. **Several signals were counted that are not evidence.** An estimated debt (a county assessment used as a placeholder) was scored as a recorded debt: 49% of `recorded_debt` signals were estimates (F1, fixed and committed this morning as 4bc5792, effective at the next rescore). The generic `distressed` tag gives PROPERTY credit to records that are not physical distress: 23,722 leads carry it, and 1,578 of the WARM and HOT leads reach stack 2 only because of it (A3). Name-only matches (jail, bankruptcy, divorce, name-resolved probate) count exactly like court records (F6); court divorce hits measure 34% corroborated, 21% proven wrong person, 45% unverified (A5).
3. **Freshness is overstated and the refresh machinery is mostly dead.** 85% of rows show `last_seen` within 30 days, but the full pipeline has not written the board since 2026-08-29 (three OOM kills, one 15-hour run refused by the count guard), the daily job refreshes 2,207 rows (1.3%), and per-source health in `run_meta` is frozen at 8/29 (O2, O4). Nineteen sources holding 18,284 rows are 80% or more stale.
4. **Breadth outside the footprint is thin.** Distressed leads are in scope in all 146 counties, but half the state (73 counties) has one distress family, 16 counties have 25 rows or fewer, three have none, and the largest counties outside the footprint (Mecklenburg, Charleston, Wake, Horry, Brunswick) have zero HOT leads between them.

**One time-boxed emergency.** `docs/listings.json.gz` is 84.3 MiB and growing 2 to 4 MiB a day. GitHub rejects a file over 100 MiB. When it crosses, every publisher's push fails, prints a message, and exits 0, and the oversized blob stays in local history and blocks later pushes. Estimated 4 to 8 days (O1). Mitigated this morning: a pre-commit gate (36428c5) refuses any commit containing a file over 95 MiB, so history stays clean and later pushes keep working. It stops the damage, not the growth: when the file reaches 95 MiB publishing pauses until the payload is split or trimmed, which is the real fix.

**What is sound.** The distinct-category stack design, the ARV trust gate and its three call sites, `retract_equity_rank`, the board lock protocol, the count guard (it refused four bad writes correctly), per-file atomic writes, backup pruning, the Pages pipeline (0 failures in the last 100 deploys), and the divorce role and recency rules. Section 13.

**Owner decisions already made are not re-opened here.** Public repository visibility, the PII disclosure drafts, and the land max-bid default were answered on 2026-09-21 and are outside this register.

## 2. Scope, method and limits

- **Two scopes.** Flip-type leads (scheduled foreclosure, sheriff or HOA sale, auction, REO) are in scope in the 18 footprint counties. Distressed leads are in scope in all 146 NC and SC counties (`config.in_scope_distressed`, owner direction 2026-09-15). I had treated 18 counties as the whole scope until today; the coverage ledger said the other 90K rows were out of scope by direction, which was wrong for distressed leads. It is corrected in `docs/COVERAGE_100_LEDGER_2026-09-20.md`.
- **Method.** Streaming passes over the board (`board_stream.iter_board_rows`, no full load), run while a scheduled job held the machine near its swap limit, so I ran them one at a time. The two agents read code and ran synthetic `Listing` objects through the real functions; they did not load the board. Prevalence numbers in the agents' reports are quoted from other files unless noted.
- **Free and public data only.** No paid APIs, no CAPTCHA, login or WAF bypass. A robots.txt Disallow is not treated as a wall. A cell that needs one of the walled things is reported as walled.
- **What "seen in 30 days" means.** `last_seen` is set when a `Listing` is built (`default_factory=utcnow`) and takes the max on merge. Any ingest script that rebuilds a row refreshes it. It says a process wrote the row recently, not that the source still lists the record. Treat every freshness figure here as an upper bound.
- **Not done.** A reconciliation of the scraper registry (209 registered scrapers, per the last full-run log) against the 156 sources on the board: my first attempt used the wrong registry API and I did not redo it. Which registered scrapers produce zero rows is therefore unknown (A7).

## 7. Signals and scoring

The scorer collects signals as (name, category, weight), keeps the best weight per category, and counts distinct categories as the stack. HOT needs stack 2 or more, equity at or above the median, a mailable owner and no senior-lien risk. WARM is stack 2 or more, or score 28 or more with equity, or an absentee owner with stack 1 and score 20 or more. The design of one-category-per-kind is sound. What goes into it is not always evidence. Full detail with file and line references is in `docs/audit_signal_logic_2026-09-21.md`; the ones that change what a bidder sees:

## 13. Checked and found sound

- Distinct-category stack with best weight per category. Liens and repeat filings do not triple-count; the foreclosure chain lis pendens, court sale, upset bid and foreclosure sale collapses into one FINANCIAL category.
- ARV trust gate: one definition used by calc, equity and the tier band. `retract_equity_rank` copies before editing and re-derives through the single tier function, so a late retraction cannot re-promote to HOT.
- `sold_confirmed` excludes a parcel from scoring; `_active_only` grace is reasoned against the statute; the board-quality pass withholds the verdict on day one after a sale date and refuses to set `sold_confirmed` on a date alone.
- Court divorce role filter, attorney and guardian exclusion, recency decay, and the middle-initial conflict rule (16 tests).
- `fullmer_rank` never drops a lead, refuses to treat judgments or opening bids as tax arrears, and avoids double-counting one death.
- Board lock protocol (mkdir-atomic, stale-break by rename, shell and Python implementations tested against each other), the count guard (refused four bad writes correctly and fails safe), per-file atomic writes (the 9/17 disk-full event did not corrupt the live board), backup pruning (regression-tested), the Pages pipeline (0 failures in the last 100 deploys), the parcel cache refresh (26 of 26 counties on 9/20, atomic replace), and the repo-size watchdog (it worked; nobody acted).
