## Not worth it, and why

**Paid contact/list SaaS in the same tier as the existing stack, no new capability:** REISift / REIsift, DataSift, DataFlik, PropStream, BatchData, BatchLeads, BatchDialer, BatchSkipTracing, BeenVerified, SmartSkip.io, Invelo, LandGlide, Land Insights, BiggerPockets Pro, Gab Wireless. Several are the guest's own product. Take the disclosed *scoring recipes* and filter specs; do not buy the subscription.

**Paid per-report vendors that are enrichment gates, not discovery:** ProTitleUSA / ProTitle USA, DocSource USA, death-certificate and title-search ordering, Baker Private Investigations / BPI Truthfinders, "The Bakers" PI firm. ProTitle is a plausible paid bypass of the SCDOT / Public Index / AcclaimWeb walls but no residential price was ever disclosed on air.

**Texas and Florida-only, no NC/SC analogue:** Linebarger and Perdue, MVBA, McCreary Veselka Bragg & Allen (the transferable part is structural, extract the plaintiff's-attorney field, not the vendor); adverse-possession annotation in the assessor legal description (Fla. Stat. 95.18 has no NC or SC counterpart); DC weekly 5+ unit sale-price reports and the entire TOPA multifamily mechanic; mineral and extractive-operator referrals, Pirkey Power Plant; vexatious-litigant registries (the reproducible version is a filings-count by party name off the eCourts JSON).

**Confirmed dead at any realistic budget:** Tyler Technologies / Odyssey bulk docket access, ~$250k integration plus ~$70k/yr with unclear scope. Useful byproduct only: NC is named as one of the handful of states running a single statewide docket, which is a structural advantage for the NC lane.

**Bot-walled or login-walled, already known:** Zillow FSBO/FRBO, Zillow + realtor.com blended into a mailer offer, Newspapers.com, Ancestry as a *discovery* source. Note the split verdict on Ancestry: as a manual VA-operated verification and tree-building tool it is cheap and two operators run it in-house successfully (069), including finding heirs in Mexico and North Carolina, and it displaces the $10-30k retained-genealogist path. It is still not a scrape and not a lead feed. The two mechanics worth a VA's hands are the Shoebox (re-anchor on the confirmed decedent so you don't rebuild the wrong tree) and messaging the owner of an existing family tree, including a private one.

**Not public in NC or SC:** utility shutoff records, police incident *narratives* (NC G.S. 132-1.4 and SC FOIA 30-4-50(a)(8) shield them, so the TX phone-number gold does not transfer; front-page repeat-call data is still public and still a signal), LPR databases and vehicle registration (DPPA plus NCDMV/SCDMV, buy it as a PI capability), property-manager-of-record collecting rent on a dead owner's parcel, payment plans set up by phone with the plaintiff's attorney.

**Marketing channels misfiled as sources:** nationwide PPC/Google/Meta inbound, Facebook lead ads to a GoHighLevel voice agent, YouTube local-market channels, WeBuyHouses.com franchise and third-party lead vendors (their own count: 70% junk), New Western (a dispo counterparty whose agents lowball as policy), listing-agent referral networks, insurance-claim triggers, "Adventures in Commercial Real Estate" course. PPC specifically does not work for this asset class: $20,000 burned over four months with no viable pipeline, because half these owners do not know a problem exists and are not searching for a solution.

**Transcription artifacts, do not wire a domain off the quote:** "grave site.com" (probably FindAGrave or BillionGraves), "Bynchano Association" (treat as a nudge to widen HOA-lien coverage past Charleston), "Land Freedom Code", "two people's search" (probably TruePeopleSearch, verify before wiring), the "elderly list" whose supplier is never named.

**Already built, do not rebuild:** Google Earth plus paid boots-on-ground photo service, qPublic / Schneider Geospatial for sale price and sqft, the jail rosters themselves, the SoS registered-agent enricher. The net-new work on all four is the *join* or the *persistence*, not the scraper.

## Numeric rules that should change our scoring

**Delinquency is not a 2-3 year window.** Make it a per-state parameter equal to statutory time-to-auction minus about a year, and score it as a monotonic ramp with **no upper bound**. Year 1 is worthless, 2 is interested, 3 better, 4 plus penalties is peak (048). Ep 070's production buy box is 2-6 years. Ep 054's fractured-heirship band is 10-20 years, and he paid just under $80,000 on 17 years unpaid, precisely because the county's contract firm will not sue where serving 100+ heirs exceeds the fee. Ep 061 has owners 10+ years behind whom the county has never touched, and they are numb to notices. Add the production dollar filter that actually gets worked: **$10,000+ delinquent AND zero payments in the last 24 months** (034). Keep seven rolls and flag intermittent patterns.

**Owner count is a hard cost driver with a ceiling.** Current operator consensus, including Fullmer's own revision: **four or less** is the target, 1-5 the buy box, degrade sharply past 6-7 unless value exceeds roughly $500k, hard pass at 10+ except at $1M-$2.5M value with taxes under $100-200k. Reject outright at roughly **$100,000 of litigation and 4 years**. Count **probates, not heirs** (three post-deceased heirs each with a spouse and three kids is 12 heirs and 3 probates). Treat any heir count as "statutory, unverified" until prior marriages and children outside marriage are checked: one deal booked as a clean one-heir widow turned into four heirs. First-call counts understate by an order of magnitude ("just me" resolving to 13). Compute the margin kill at intake: **(heirs x per-heir price) + taxes + curative**, which is what killed a ~$75k lot with 23-24 heirs at $500-750 each plus ~$20k taxes.

**Per-owner price: $500-$2,000 is the floor for indifferent heirs, not the band.** Operating range is $1,000 to $5,000 with a hard $1,000 minimum and never a free or gifted interest (kills the later "they never paid me" claim). Anchor at $2,000 and expect to clear at 2.5-3x. Fullmer's own correction was $15,000 down to $5,000, not down to $500. Deliberate 5-10x overpayment on slivers is correct where it unlocks the block (paid 5x on 0.34%, picked up $150,000 of equity and got everyone else cheap; $5,000 to unlock $90-100k). Above that: $37,000 to an anchor heir vs $700 to distant heirs in the same family, $45,000 for a single 20% interest as an entry position, $83,000 for a 62.5% block. Quote **net** to the seller and never present a combined family number.

**Curative cost is at least three tiers, not a fixed $10-30k.** Tier 1, sub-$1,000: $150-175 to release a paid-off institutional mortgage; ~$500 for a dissolved-LLC affidavit; ~$800 for a non-litigated fractional close; $160 for template review where you send the attorney's own form with only price highlighted. Tier 2, low thousands, usually paid on the HUD rather than out of pocket: $3,250 per probate, $4-5k typical, up to ~$15k for three probates on one deal; $2,000 filing plus $800-1,200 publication as the only up-front cash. Tier 3, the ~20% with judicial involvement: this is where the $10-30k figure belongs. Tail: mediation is the cost cliff ($10k month one, ~$5k/month after, ~$80k at nine months, ~$90k at twelve and past the point of no return), and roughly 20 break-even deals at ~$50k out of thousands. Two structural notes that break the "margin > 2-5x a fixed number" gate outright: counsel will take **25% of backend** instead of a fee, and filing fees are **tiered on property value**, so cheap parcels are cheap to litigate. Assign a cost tier to the lead, then set the profit floor from that tier, rather than applying one global floor.

**Cents-on-value is risk-adjusted, and the denominator changes by class.** ~15 cents on a remainder-only interest; 20-35 cents on fractured many-owner; 33-40 on a single-heir defect; **50-60 on court-confirmed clean 100%** (buying one person's ownership with no fighting parties drops the risk far enough to pay them more). On industrial, ep 036 paid **190-210% of CAD** and netted $450,000 in 18 days, which means **do not knock out a commercial, industrial or land parcel because delinquent taxes exceed assessed value**; that apparent negative equity is the moat. Alternate denominators actually used: post-split value on splittable land, residual/end-use on commercial land, and gross exit price (29-35% all-in) on litigated files. Also note ep 034 abandons cents-on-value entirely for **3-5x on cash out of pocket with a $50,000 absolute profit floor**.

**Contactability ratios are channel-specific and the baseline 1:8-1:12 sits between two regimes.** Bulk dialer answer rate 3-5% (1:20 to 1:33). Manually worked distressed records ~20% (1:5). Premium skip-trace phone accuracy tops out ~80%, cheap tier ~30%, and the regulated/unregulated head-to-head is a coin flip. Budget 3-5 numbers per property on multi-owner records, up to 20 per lead. Cadence beats volume: run the same numbers 4-6 times over three days, 11am-2pm and again at 6pm; escalate out of the bulk lane after 3-4 failed dialer attempts; ceiling 7-10 touches then rest the lead 6-8 months. Re-skip every 1-3 years, faster for distressed. Do not dial from VOIP (carrier blocking cost one shop six months and took them from 27 deals/yr to 14-15). Phone-live gate: did the number receive a text or call in the last 30 days. Trigger on the skip-trace **last-seen date**, not the presence of a number. Roughly **50% of first contacts happen through a third party**, so that is half the pipeline, not a fallback. Funnel to hard-code: 1,000 owners produce ~400 correct numbers and ~150 live conversations; every 100 dials fully exhausts ~5 homeowners, and an exhausted record is the **promotion trigger** to the deep-prospecting team, not trash. And invert the sign on the hardest cases: **5 attempts with no valid number is HIGH priority**, because the owner is probably deceased or the record names the wrong owner and every competitor is hitting the same wall.

**Leads per deal: nobody reports 30 leads.** 1,200 fully researched leads produced 82 contracts (15:1) with **more than half never contacted by a human**; 25 docket-sourced leads produced 2 deals (12.5:1); disciplined callers close 1 in 5 *conversations* while poor targeting needs ~30 conversations; inbound runs 1:10. Target 1-in-3 acceptance once the buy box is tight. Tag the 30:1 figure as conversations-at-bad-targeting and stop treating lead supply as the constraint.

**Research time box, before any dial: 5-15 minutes.** Ep 070 says 5-10, ep 049 says 10 maximum and both men name it as the single behavior change that mattered, ep 033 says 15-20, ep 053 allows 45 minutes at ~$200k exposure. Front-loading research is what killed ep 061's pipeline. Escalate to paid genealogy only when the property is clearly abandoned **and** there is no obituary, which itself indicates a broken family.

**Cycle time and inventory mix.** Target 120-150 days first contact to proceeds, 4 turns a year. Any forced sale is **6-8 months minimum** (judgment ~90 days, writ two weeks, three sheriff notices over 30-45 days, ~2 months to sale, ~30 days to clear title). Partition is 9-12 months best case, 2 years typical, 2.5 on a flagship. Cap heavy-curative and long-game files at **10% of inventory**, or run Fullmer's 50/50 split where fast turns fund the carry on the messes. Watch the leading indicator: 20% of a 150-unit inventory aged past a year, and almost all the stragglers involved a judge. Nurture window is ~4 months at light touch every two weeks; recycle uncontactable leads monthly; a 14-day text reply lag closed a deal, so do not kill a texted lead at 7 days; **heirship has no statute of limitations, so an heirship lead never ages out and age-of-death must not decay its score.**

**Base rates worth hard-coding.** Only ~30% of deaths ever produce a probate filing, which is the whole arithmetic case for the obituary and "Estate of" lanes. ~50% of files reach 100% ownership by the time probate is filed. Two-thirds of deals need no litigation. Unrecorded-creditor risk is ~0.3% (6 of 2,000+ probates), so stop discounting for phantom debt and penalize only recorded liens and judgments. 95-98% of litigation settles, so underwrite the 50/50 split-the-baby, not the win. At least 10% of live leads in any CRM have already sold to someone else, and re-enriching a 2023-24 dataset newly flagged 170 properties of which 25 (14.7%) had already sold, so old rows are unripe rather than dead. Evidence of a competing fractional buyer appeared twice in "hundreds and hundreds" of calls (<1%). 2.5% of obituaries have a deed recorded in the 90 days before; 4% sell to a non-family member in the 90 days after (a lost-deal detector, not a lead). Roughly 1 in 20 SFR deeds carries an extra platted lot; roughly 1 in 5 "landlocked" parcels is not.

**Score thresholds and funnel sizing.** Back-tested against 200 known closed deals, real deals landed above the **90th percentile** of the distress score and production was loosened to the **85th**. A 40/100 cut removed 60% of a 100,000-row pull with little deal loss. A large county raw pull is ~300,000 parcels and a correctly filtered call list off it should be **200-500 units**, not thousands. The sanity check on over-filtering: of 453,000 records, exactly **seven** were 3-years-delinquent AND titled "Estate of", about 1.5 per 100,000. The sharpest filter is a call-today queue, not a business.

**New scoring inputs volunteered against the ep-019 frame.** Add code liens as their own class, distinct from judgments and tax liens. Add **access and speed-to-locate a missing or adverse cotenant** as its own TIME factor (Fullmer agreed to bolt it on). Add count of owners on the deed and length of ownership (both possibly underweighted today). Add rollback/deferred tax as a field, because it changes both capital requirement and urgency. Add heir mailing address in-county vs out-of-state, which is the observable proxy behind a 53x price spread inside one family. Tag liens **person-attached vs property-attached and do not sum them into one encumbrance total**. Add facility type for incarcerated owners. Add a mandatory owner profile before any deal is discussed: age, occupation, wealth tier, where they live, litigiousness, likelihood of retaining counsel. Both Burke and Fullmer call that profile the weakest link in their current process.

**Confidence and verification flags.** Never trust a vacancy field: mark it UNVERIFIED until physically checked (ep 061's "vacant" parcel was occupied by a hiding heir, found only when a locksmith was sent). CAD improvement flags cannot gate vacancy or SFR-vs-land: one appraisal district showed a house on the subject parcel that actually sat on the neighbour's. On acreage, an old structure **falsely elevates** the automated value, so an improvement flag should reduce value confidence on land, not raise it. Obituary relationships are probabilistic: a named "son" was not an heir. Two conflicting DOBs on one TLO record means a merged identity, penalize match confidence. A name-to-person resolver needs middle initial plus death-date logic before merging (a same-name father/son differing by middle initial scattered a skip trace across two states). And per ep 033 with Fullmer endorsing it on air, a **LOW-confidence value band should not knock a lead out** if the low bound already clears fixed curative several times over, which is the opposite of tightening on valuation confidence.

**Capital flags.** Pre-suit, the whole tax balance rides to closing and out of pocket is only buyouts plus $10-15k legal. Post-suit or sale-set, you must fund the full balance immediately, and a $40,000 balance cost one operator a deal to a JV. Those are two different products needing two capital flags, not one ranked list. Taxes are otherwise a HUD deduction, not cash to close (paid the week before sale, $60-80k held back on a $250k all-in file), except where the advance is deliberately the leverage instrument. Capital concentration cap: a single ~$600k acquisition consumes 4-6 bread-and-butter deals' worth of capital, so eight $125k-basis properties beat one $1M property. Stop buying while 30% of the credit line remains. Cost of capital: always take bank money at 4-8%; JV equity annualizes into the hundreds of percent.

**Marketing and locate economics.** Marketing target ~8% of deal size ($4,500/contract against $60k average). Origination ran ~$800/contract on land vs $2,000-3,000 on SFH. The curative lane runs at effectively **$0 marketing** versus ~$10,000/month for land direct mail; cost moves from mail into data work and phone labor. $3,000 into tax-delinquent plus absentee mail returned ~$150,000 over 2-3 months. And the biggest single correction: **locate cost is a rounding error, not a curative line item.** PI hourly is $100-200 not $80-350, the largest single digital-locate bill ever was $300, $5,000 all-in clears ~90% of hard-to-find owners, US process service ~$150, boots-on-street ~$1,500. If locate is 1-5% of curative rather than a major share, the engine should spend far more freely on contactability than the current model implies. Small-money field spend that works: $20 to an Uber Eats driver to confirm an occupant by photo, $100 on a canvass flyer, $500 flat to a neighbor for a phone number. Note the field-safety rule: ep 047 reversed ten years of his own practice after a pistol was drawn during a drive-by, and now runs **zero unauthorized site visits**.

## Commercial / industrial / multifamily / land

Direct answer to the operator's question: **the engine does not cover these classes today, and the corpus says two of the three should be covered as cheap computable flags on the existing residential spine rather than as their own facets.** The reason is dispo, not sourcing. Ep 028 inverted 12 acquisition / 3 dispo staff to 1 / 19 in 60 days to survive, framed as "hire 20 or file bankruptcy." Commercial and land produce inventory you cannot exit without a specialist channel, and Fullmer's own in-house agents tried and abandoned both: "there's things that you have to go get agents at other places for farm and ranch because you actually do need that network," and a 10-acre-plus manufactured-home listing at $400k+ was never sold and was the most time-expensive listing they ever ran.

**Land is the highest-margin and least-contested class in the corpus, and several of the best individual deals came from it.** 65 acres, $1M profit on $250k. 26 acres bought at $405k, split, house and 5 acres for $345k plus 22 acres for ~$345k, $220k profit in 90 days. 3.5 commercial acres from a defunct LLC, basis ~$200-210k, LOI in three days at $1,887,000. $40k in on a hoarder's throwaway parcels, ~$163k net in 30 days. 140 of 186 acres out of a partition suit, $275,000 all-in against roughly $1.5M realized. Everyone chases structures, so vacant lots are uncontested.

What to build for land, all cheap and mostly Tier A: frontage threshold table per jurisdiction, subdividability field, landlocked as rank-up, acreage mismatch, plat-cluster dead subdivisions, ag/PUV rollback, land-value ratio with denser neighbors, cemetery layer, adjacency to the existing buyer registry. Ep 061 gives the sharpest geography gate: ~5-acre parcels within about an hour of a metro core, split into four one-acre lots, in markets absorbing manufactured homes and spec builds. That maps directly onto the Asheville and Greenville-Spartanburg one-hour rings.

Land warnings that must be encoded, because they all cut against the residential rules: **land dispo is the abutter list at intake, not the MLS.** Land resale is roughly **2x cost**, not 85-90 cents ($5-7k buys on $15-20k lots reselling at $15-16k in 30 days), so land tolerates a far wider bid-ask. Land comps are much weaker than SFR, so price on geography, dirt vs paved access, access rights, water, and attrition rate. **Wetlands are invisible in standard mapping layers** and one shop repeatedly bought unbuildable parcels because of it, so never score buildability from GIS alone. Rural land liquidity is rate-sensitive and evaporates first: a $900k-appraised tract, ~$350k all-in, meant to exit in four months, carried 12 months at $3,500/month and sold at $750k when the buyer pool went from tons of showings to begging someone to look. **Treat any 2023-24 land comp as stale by roughly 40%**: two independent marks, $35M bulk down to $20M and a $28M expectation contracting at ~$15M, and Fullmer's own Arizona subdivision underwritten by homebuilders at $25-28M now valued at $15M. Land was also dying as a *mail channel* as of 2024-26, margins compressed at $10k/month marketing, "literally making no money on land at all." The land worst case is unique and useful: if you buy a drainage ditch by mistake, stop paying the taxes and let it go to tax sale.

**Commercial and industrial need a separate valuation path and a mandatory DD gate.** CAD is invalid as a denominator in both directions: one appraisal district said $250,000 while the delinquent tax bill was $450-500,000, so everyone read it as underwater, and the deal sold for $1,000,000 netting ~$450,000 in 18 days. Another carried a Dallas industrial lot below its own $425,000 tax bill while two brokers priced it $1.5M and $2M and a neighbor paid ~$1M in a week. Value commercial land by **residual**: end use, cost to build, work backwards, what remains is the land. The one genuinely scraper-actionable commercial heuristic is ep 070's occupancy discriminator: delinquent **plus evidence of an active tenant** means someone with control is pocketing rent and not paying taxes, which is almost always a fiduciary failure by an executor, co-heir or agent, so the counterparty is a court-supervisable human; delinquent **with no tenant** is unstabilized or abandoned and a weaker deal. Detect from commercial/flex/industrial parcel + multi-year delinquency + business licence, utility, signage or occupancy signal.

Commercial DD gate, all public/GIS fields, done in a weekend before an auction (053): DOT gore geometry for a driveway entrance, flood zone, plat restrictions including greenway dedication, water and sewer main location plus parcel elevation relative to sewer, zoning map and permitted uses, neighboring subdivision deed restrictions, and the core question, why has this never been developed. Fullmer states plainly that they do almost no DD on residential and that commercial requires this. Add: dock height that is neither true 48-inch nor grade-level is unleasable to both tenant types; environmental kills a $3M building unless the buyer is fund-scale; nonconforming-use and overlay risk is first-class here in a way it never is residentially (a city adopted an overlay, the building became legally nonconforming, and the owner under-leased "for pennies" for 15 years).

Commercial timing and liquidity, which is where the four-week rule dies: 8 hours to underwrite one commercial asset vs a day or two for a 30-year residential report; lease-up has roughly tripled from 3 months to 6-9, and 10-14k sqft sat vacant a full year in a 3% vacancy market; vacant lease-up runs 12-18 months at zero cash flow, which is why one operator caps himself at one vacant industrial building at a time; one home-run commercial deal every **18 months** even working the market hard, so do not model commercial on residential lead-to-deal ratios; make-ready is mandatory because brokers refuse to work a dirty building. Liquidity scale for calibration: Dallas holds ~1.2 billion sqft of industrial and San Antonio ~150 million ("this is like a neighborhood in Dallas"); Greenville-Spartanburg and Asheville are an order of magnitude below San Antonio, which means **10,000-30,000 sqft owner-user product is the only liquid industrial size in this footprint**, and the owner-user is the buyer who pays the most and tolerates functional flaws. Any commercial lead the engine emits needs ep 042's submission schema or it gets ignored: the building, the seller's story and situation, estimated capex, estimated achievable rent, and a price. Address alone is worthless.

**Multifamily is uniformly negative across all 47 episodes and there is no MF sourcing content anywhere.** The three positive data points are not multifamily plays at all: an 8-plex at ~$250,000 against ~$800-850k is the incarcerated-owner lane (owner at FCI Fort Dix, contact approval 6-8 weeks, quarter million wired to the son, NY requiring 13 documents, a Texas probate, a refused NY ancillary probate and a quiet title suit as fallback); a duplex netting $10,000 came in through the code-enforcement lane; ground-up 32 units on a scraped San Diego duplex is development, not distress. Against them: a D-class rural 8-unit targeted for break-even and exit; mobile home parks exited over monthly eviction churn and manager failure; a 2021-vintage San Antonio apartment deal going back to the bank on adjustable debt; ~20% lost on a 2021 Arizona build-to-rent; "long-term rentals are a myth, cash flow is a lie" from an operator who scaled to 35-40 doors; and "commercial cash flow is a myth on a portfolio too" from one who sold his rent houses for warehouses. The one 2-4 unit bull case is explicitly gated on metros *transitioning into* major-metro status at ~2.2M people, so on the guest's own logic it does not transfer to Greenville-Spartanburg (~950k) or Asheville (~475k).

The only defensible MF facet is a bounded cohort inference: join apartment and 2-4 unit parcels with **2020-2022 purchase dates** against substitute-trustee and notice-of-hearing filings, deeds in lieu, and 2020-2022 deeds of trust with maturity or ARM language, in Buncombe, Henderson, Greenville and Spartanburg. Small MF will otherwise surface incidentally through the code-enforcement lane.

Two land/commercial plays worth flagging as opportunistic rather than pipeline: the **FEMA pad-lease** on destroyed mobile home parks (13 pads bought at ~$110-120k all-in on owner financing, FEMA did the cleanup, supplied and set trailers, placed and removed tenants, maintained everything and paid monthly rent, then auctioned the trailers at ~$2,500 each, "a brand new mobile home park on the government's dime"), which is the only content in the corpus adjacent to post-Helene WNC; and **big-box retail vacancy to self-storage** (Walmart, Kmart, Bed Bath shells, 1,200-1,600 units), which is detectable in no deed, tax, foreclosure or probate record and would need retail closure announcements or business-licence lapse.

## Contradictions left standing

These are unresolved on the record and each one is a design decision the operator has to make. Nobody in the corpus reconciles them.

1. **Lead quality or conversion?** Ep 041 kills 90-95% of leads on paper before anyone dials and runs 5-20 calls a month producing 5-7 deals. Ep 040, same podcast, argues "it's not a lead problem, it's a conversion problem," does zero distress sourcing (no tax list, no probate, no foreclosure, no title work) and did 118 deals in 12 months. Both are producing.
2. **Chase chaos or chase consolidability?** Ep 025: chain depth is the moat, "one obituary, three siblings" is commodity work for juniors, and the 10-12 owner chains are the high-margin low-competition tier. Ep 031's closed deal is the exact opposite profile, consolidate to 100% from a single owner and get the attorney to insure the chain. Ep 058 would have walked if the trail had gone to uncles and cousins. That argues for scoring "can this be consolidated to 100%?" as a value driver rather than scoring for chaos.
3. **Fractional pricing philosophy: equal, attachment-weighted, or percentage-of-pro-rata?** $5,000 flat per heir regardless of percentage, held even when a 15% owner objects that a 6% owner got the same. Versus $37,000 to the anchor heir and $700 to distant heirs. Versus 30-50% of that owner's pro-rata perceived retail. Three incompatible philosophies from operators with the same coach.
4. **Treat each owner in a silo, or work the person across their whole portfolio?** Ep 032 worked one heir as a single relationship covering four properties in three states and now asks every contacted heir what else they own. Ep 033's entire pricing model is cross-heir comparison inside one family. Ep 038 refuses to divide or combine.
5. **Pay the taxes up front or let the HUD pay them?** Ep 027 pays 100% of the bill and sends the receipt, because the advance is what manufactures the leverage the demand letter runs on. Eps 024, 030 and 061 say never from working capital: ep 061 bought heir interests, never paid the taxes, listed, and the closing paid them off, $15k in and $90k out. A model treating taxes as up-front cash overstates capital need and rejects viable deals.
6. **Big-MSA liquidity gate: sourcing filter, exit filter, or something you manufacture?** Ep 025 rejects it for lead selection outright ("I don't care, I want to see lawsuits"). Ep 054 has nine profitable years in a 60,000-person county and Fullmer concedes on air that his stay-away rule is a high-volume constraint that inverts with a tight buy box. Ep 049 says higher-value markets have fewer defects because curing already became economical. Ep 041 violated the gate twice and won by *creating* demand (650 post-Katrina houses, buyers bused in from three states, recruited an exclusive hard-money lender). Ep 067 has Fullmer doing deals in Ulster County NY specifically because nobody operates there. Against all of that, ep 048 would exclude low-value interiors on $50k-house arithmetic and ep 039 argues from effort-invariance for a minimum value floor, which ep 038 then contradicts by observing that expensive properties simply do not go delinquent. **WNC and Upstate SC are not big MSAs, so this contradiction is directly load-bearing for us.**
7. **Is the delinquent-tax list even the primary source?** Ep 030, from an operator doing 140 deals a year: no list exists for distress, and their best distressed deal arrived inbound. Ep 024 says the list actively lies (entity owner + large balance + code liens + no pending foreclosure is the signature of an investor deliberately carrying debt to closing, and they got cold-emailed off their own live listing). Ep 048 demotes it to parity with virtual driving for dollars. Ep 063 does not age-filter it at all. Eps 025, 026, 034 and 070 still run it as the spine.
8. **Contactability: a constraint to solve or a signal to score up?** Ep 026 inverts the sign entirely (five failed attempts is high priority). Ep 025 says occupant and tenant phone enrichment is unwinnable at any price, twenty disconnected prepaid numbers per person, and door-knocking replaced it. Ep 033's two best heirs were structurally unreachable by phone, one with a disabled line and one who had blocked her.
9. **The assessor owner field: unreliable lag or free death detector?** Ep 024 says CAD does not update for months so a name-based lead may already be sold, prefer the recorded-deed date. Ep 030 uses a change in that same field as its death detector. Both may hold and they pull the resolver in opposite directions.
10. **Quiet title: universal cleanup tool or overturnable order?** Fullmer, hard: "you can't quiet title grandma's interest away," and anyone who claims to have done it got a challengeable order. Ep 048: sue the defunct lender, no answer, release of mortgage, 60 days flat, after the note had been assigned through ~12 banks to an HSBC that had closed US operations. Different targets, and the episodes never reconcile them.
11. **Affidavit of heirship vs probate, and which one the underwriter will insure.** Ep 039 names NC as an affidavit state where an heir's deed vests today. Ep 049 says FL and CA force full probate, which is what makes timelines long, and ep 063 says FL forbids affidavits outright. Ep 061 reports Arizona title companies **refusing to insure deals that went through full court-supervised probate** after a class action, demanding an affidavit instead, and his fix was "I just moved title companies." Ep 057 closed on two affidavits for a homeless untraceable heir by switching closing companies. So this is company-specific, not statutory, and it can invert.
12. **Do old defects settle for cents?** Baseline says yes on judgment liens. Ep 054 says the opposite for title errors: courts strongly resist disturbing long-settled title, so age makes an error harder to overturn, not cheaper. Ep 050 adds that improper notice violates constitutional rights and therefore has **no statute of limitations**, so that class never ages into safety. Ep 036 buys judgments as a **debt buyer** and self-releases them inside the statute ($500 on a $4,000 judgment at 8 years, 12.5 cents) without disclosing they attach to real estate.
13. **Score the 10-item frame first, or call first and verify before paying out?** Ep 061 says front-loading research killed his pipeline; Fullmer agrees, citing coaching clients "digging way too deep, they have nothing to research yet." Ep 063 sits at the opposite pole with routinely 40+ hours on one property and 100 on another, and names knowing when to stop as the skill she personally lacks.
14. **Private or individual note holder: motivated counterparty or knockout?** Eps 041, 042 and 049 treat a natural-person deed-of-trust beneficiary as the contactable, more motivated party, and one shop emailed the note-holder's lawyer to buy the note. Ep 048 wrote off a $7k deed because the private lender owed $180k refused $20k then $80k on principle with foreclosure pending six years, and concludes you should score a long-dormant foreclosure **down** when the holder is a person. Institutional and dead lenders settle for cents; a live individual may never settle at any price.
15. **The as-is rule.** Ep 037: "we're still rehabbing almost everything," ~70% of the business historically fix-and-flip. Ep 034's first curative deal made **more absolute money** flipped, $75,000 net over 3.5 months versus an estimated $40,000 as-is in under 30 days, and that is after a $15,000 rehab budget blew out to $55,000 on a house bought sight-unseen off a 10-year-old Street View. Ep 056's operator sells **above** asking 90% of the time and deliberately sets new comps, and Fullmer corroborates with his own "Barbie house" and generalizes that premium product supports premium pricing at any price point. Ep 066 gets Fullmer to concede curb appeal on owner-user commercial ($9,600 painting roll-up doors black). The rule survives on time and risk; the raw dollars in several episodes went the other way.
16. **Obituary presence: filter or the wrong end of the distribution?** Tyler's entire model is obituary-anchored and his overlay lifted marketable properties 15%. Fullmer on the same mic: "I bet half the time I don't see an o-bit," because the people who file obituaries were the connected families, and the deepest chains are precisely the estranged ones. Absence must never demote a record on his book and must be the filter on Tyler's.
17. **The 90-day window vs years of ripening.** Tyler's headline filter is obituary plus no probate within 90 days. Fullmer says his inventory falls entirely outside that: "you need substantial time, like years." Two different cash-conversion cycles, neither resolved.
18. **Investor-density geography.** Tyler found 93% of one $50k/month mailer's closings inside the top 10 zips and neighborhoods and halved that budget on the finding. Fullmer on the same method applied to his own book: "we would fall outside of the ballpark," because curative deals are by definition the properties no investor has touched.
19. **Lien treatment.** Ep 060's guest de-noises by verifying a lien attaches to the parcel not a resident; Fullmer wants every personal judgment listed anyway because in his pre-foreclosure lane "that list right there is as important as any, that's our to-do list." Same episode: lien priority by recording date is unsafe as a default (tax deed supersedes all, TX property-tax loans jump to first, a Nevada HOA foreclosure wipes first and second). And: buying from the owner ahead of the sale **leaves the juniors alive** because only the foreclosure wipes them, which Fullmer does not treat as a problem because negotiating those away is his business. Same property, same lien stack, completely different encumbrance total depending on acquisition path.
20. **Federal tax liens.** Fullmer: "I usually won't do it, the reason is time, unless it's extremely high value." The guest immediately: "We love those properties... it's a very standard process, standard forms, you don't need to negotiate much," with the mechanic being a partial release from the property only, roughly $10,000 to release on a $1M property, conditioned on the lien not being enormous relative to value and the owner "not a big criminal."
21. **Should this engine exist at all?** Ep 058, a full segment: Fullmer's people run Podio plus a delinquent tax list plus minimal research plus phone calls and made $300-600k in a quarter, "and I hear they've been working on developing it and they're spending all their time on it." He concedes the data lane only once the pipeline funds it. His sanctioned AI use is narrow: summarize data you already have into a per-lead brief (name, DOB, obituary link, skip-trace on ~25 associated people, condensed to the 2-3 people to actually call). Ep 070 adds the boundary: machine ranks, human reads and dials; AI for obituary and land-record *searching* is where they both push back. Ep 064's guest, a career cyber/OSINT founder, argues against scraping outright: "these counties are also implementing AI kicking them out... we don't even scrape anything, we just have ladies pull it," and caps LLM document extraction at "maybe 70% there" versus a trained human. His own unaddressed ceiling: two full-time data managers cover five counties.
22. **Volume funnel or sniper shortlist?** Ep 065 picked five or six deals and worked them to exhaustion through aunts, uncles and pastors, and Fullmer endorses it. Ep 048 runs 10,000 postcards a month. Ep 053 runs one call, no engagement, next person, and his $750k deal came off a single 7-8 minute call.
23. **Cheap buyout or JV with the heir?** Ep 070 criticizes the buyout instinct: "folks get so caught up on trying to give pennies, when you could just say, let's work together." His flagship was a 60/40 split on a ~$250k property with ~$45k advanced, netting ~$80k. That makes disposition strategy a **per-owner field**, not a global one, and means an heir who refuses $1,500 may be a JV candidate rather than a dead lead.
24. **Where the binding constraint sits.** Ep 028 says disposition capacity. Ep 026 says legal support and boots on the ground. Ep 025 says attorneys of the right firm size (25 firms called before one took the work, target mid-tier firms of 100-200 attorneys, accept paying 30-40% more). Ep 038 says contact capacity. **None of them says lead volume.**

## What is still NOT known after all 70 episodes

This is the part to read twice. The corpus is deep on method and empty on this footprint.

**Nobody on 70 episodes operates in North or South Carolina.** Every state-specific claim in this brief is inference by us, not testimony. Two operators say they are "looking at" NC and TN, and ep 039 names NC and SC first in one guest's active-state list. That is the entire extent of it. Ep 048 chose NC and SC among five operating states but says nothing about their mechanics, only that he would exclude low-value interiors on house arithmetic. Nothing in the corpus validates that any of these plays works here.

**Nobody tells us whether an NC or SC title underwriter will insure a fractional interest, an affidavit-of-heirship deed, or a partial-interest position.** That single question gates the entire fractional strategy, including whether a lender will lend against a position and therefore whether the capital model works. Ep 038 says Florida underwriters will insure a 32.6% interest once probate closes and reports secondhand "I've been told up in North Carolina there's a guy that buys fractional interest with insurance there too." Secondhand, one sentence, no name, no company. Ep 061 proves the answer is company-specific and can invert overnight, and ep 050 says the real test is that no standing remains, not that the statute was followed. So the answer must be obtained from two or three named NC and SC underwriters directly, and nothing in 70 episodes shortcuts that.

**We have zero NC or SC curative-counsel benchmark.** Every legal cost figure in this brief is Texas or Florida: $400/hr, $500/hr, $3,250 per probate, $5-8k quiet title, 25% contingency, $1,500 plus $3,400 for a receiver sale, $30-40k retail estate cure. NC and SC are attorney-closing states and every closing, notary and e-recording mechanic quoted comes from a title-company state. We do not know which NC or SC firm will do this work, at what price, whether any will take backend contingency, whether flat-fee-paid-at-closing is available, or how the NC upset-bid period and the SC redemption period change the timeline. Two operators needed 25-30 firm calls to find one willing; we have made none.

**SC is structurally darker than NC and nobody addressed it.** SC runs an administrative delinquent-tax sale, which means no lawsuit, no docket, no plaintiff's attorney signature block, no return-of-service event, no party list, and no order of sale. Divorce decrees sit behind family court. SoS is captcha-walled. PublicIndex has a ToS wall. DHEC vital records are likely closed. Roughly half the sharpest signals in the Tier B list above are NC-only. Nobody in the corpus offers an administrative-sale analogue for the docket state machine, and nobody explains what replaces "named as a defendant in a tax lawsuit" in a state that does not file one.

**The negative joins all depend on an index we cannot reach.** Will deposited but no estate opened; probate open with no activity; death with no estate filed; partition and quiet title as special proceedings. Every one of these requires the NC eCourts estates and special-proceedings side, which is WAF-walled. Not one episode offers a workaround, a bulk path, or an alternate index. The manual court-export lane is the only route and nobody sizes it.

**No per-county field inventory exists, and eight separate build decisions hang on one.** Unanswered for every WNC and Upstate county: does the tax portal expose the **payer name** on a paid receipt; does it expose last-payment **date**; does any assessor carry an **in-lawsuit or status flag** (the field hunt across qPublic and lrcpwa.ncptscloud.com has not been run); does the ROD index carry a **death certificate** doc type; does the ROD index carry an **affidavit of heirship** doc type in volume; does any assessor split a bill into **multiple tax accounts per PID**; what is each county's **electronic-index start year**; is **rollback or deferred PUV** broken out as its own amount; and is **water and sewer rolled into the tax bill** (ep 060 says roughly 50% of the US attaches them to the property, which would mean the existing "taxes owed" figure is not pure ad valorem and a small water arrear could be misread as the start of tax delinquency). **Three legal mechanics are flatly unverified for NC and SC.** MERP / Medicaid estate recovery liens: federal law requires every state to run estate recovery so an analogue should exist, but the transcript is Texas-only and says nothing about NC or SC recording mechanics. Utility liens running with the land. Super-lien priority (tax deed, property-tax loans, HOA foreclosures wiping first and second). Also unaddressed: what the NC and SC equivalent of Florida's "tax certificate filed or sold" urgency state actually is, since ep 038 treats that transition as a higher tier that caps what you can pay and starts default interest burning equity.

**The resolver problem is named as a failure mode by many guests and solved by none.** Address and situs resolution, name-to-person merging (the same-name father/son case, the middle-initial requirement, death-date logic), obituary relationship reliability, geocoding legacy addresses on renamed streets. Every guest's answer is a human: call, door-knock, hire a genealogist, phone the clerk, pay a neighbor $500. **We cannot forecast lead volume for this footprint from this corpus.** No operator gives a parcel count matching any of these filters in any comparable county. **Post-Helene WNC is a blank.** The only disaster content in the corpus is Gulf Coast: the FEMA pad-lease play after Hurricane Michael, and 650 houses in three years after Katrina by skip-tracing owners who scattered and never came back. **Staffing and throughput never reconcile.** Claimed output ranges from 6 deals a year to 150, with no consistent staffing model. Nobody says what a single operator with good data can close per year, and the closest numbers (1 deal a month, 10-30 live leads, 6/yr at $300-500k) come from operators doing all their own dialing with no data engine at all.

**Ethical and legal exposure is named and never checked.** Buying a judgment as a debt buyer and self-releasing it without disclosing it is attached to real estate. Taking an executed deed at signing and holding it unrecorded as an anti-backout device. Prison commissary payment mechanics where the facility restricted account funding to the attorney rather than the investor. Paying someone else's taxes when you hold no interest, which Fullmer learned is legally a **gift**, not a lien or a claim. Mailing 1,000-piece batches to every skip-traced relative and associate without knowing the relationship. Per-heir cease-and-desist handling (one of 23 heirs sent a formal one, so suppression must be per-heir, not per-property). None of these has been checked against NC or SC law or bar rules, and several are the kind of thing that ends a program rather than costing it money.

**The excess-proceeds exit is unpriced here.** Ep 069's cleanest exit is to hand the tax attorney your affidavit and deeds, ask to be foreclosed on, and collect the surplus, with 90-97% recovery and a friendly third party bidding.
